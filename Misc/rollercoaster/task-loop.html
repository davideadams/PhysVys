Your job: build a working coaster circuit with a loop in it, using the smallest lift hill you can that still gets the train all the way round and back to the station.

Part A walks you through the builder. Do it once, then clear the track and start the real task in Part B. You will hand in an exported summary of your finished coaster along with this sheet.

Part A — Learning the builder

  1. Open the Rollercoaster Builder. In the top bar, open the Track list and choose Custom (blank). You now have nothing but a short station.
  2. Press Build to open the building window. Look for the yellow arrow at the end of the track: that is the build head, and everything you build grows from it.
  3. The Direction row chooses whether the next piece goes straight on or turns. The Slope row chooses the slope you want to be travelling at once the piece is done. Pick one of each, then press Build this.
    • You do not have to build the ramps yourself. If you are level and ask for a climb, the builder puts the transition in for you — so to keep climbing, just press Build this again.
    • Anything the track cannot do from where the arrow is now goes grey, and the reason appears under the piece name.
  4. Chain, beside the slope buttons, puts a chain lift on the pieces you build. A chain can only go on track that climbs, so it switches itself off as soon as you stop climbing.
  5. Special… opens the pieces that are not plain track: Station, Brakes, Launch, and the two Loop pieces. A loop has to start and finish on level track.
  6. Backspace removes the last piece. Clear takes the whole track back to the station.
  7. ◀◀ and ▶ walk a cursor back and forward through the track you have already built, so you can look at any piece again.
  8. Finish track tries to join the end of your track back round to the station for you. It will say so if it cannot find a way.
  9. The bar across the top of the screen shows the track length and whether what you have is a closed circuit, a shuttle, or still open.
  10. Heights in the top bar labels the track with its height above the ground. The Build window also shows the height of the build head as you go.
  11. ▶ Test sends the train off (the space bar does the same). ⟲ puts it back at the station.
  12. Save keeps a named copy of your track, so you can come back to it in a later lesson. Use your own name.

Warm-up

Build a small circuit with no loop in it — a climb, a drop, then Finish track — and run it. When it works, press Clear and start the task.

Part B — The task

Build a coaster that meets all of these:

  • It is a closed circuit: the train leaves the station and comes back to it. The status bar has to say so.
  • It has one loop in it.
  • The train gets through the loop without leaving the track, and makes it all the way home. The Report window tells you how each run ended.
  • The lift hill is as small as you can make it while all of the above is still true.
Keep it a fair test. Decide your settings in Setup before you start, write them in the box below, and do not change them again. Your answer depends on them. Between trials, change the height of the lift hill and as little else as you can.

Part C — Handing it in

  1. Make sure the last run you did was a successful one. The report always describes the most recent run.
  2. Open the Report window and press Export summary.
  3. Save the file, name it with your own name, and hand it in together with this sheet.

Part D — Questions

1. Compare your smallest lift hill with other people's. Did everyone get the same height? If not, what was different about your coaster, or your settings?

2. Use Heights to find how tall your loop is, and compare that with the height of your lift hill. What do you notice about the two numbers? Why do you think that is?

3. On a run that just made it, was there a moment when the train came closest to not getting round? Where was it, and what is your evidence? The Graphs window may help.

4. Real coasters nearly always have their lift hill as the highest point of the whole ride. From what you found, why do you think that is?

5. A designer wants the same coaster but with a taller loop. What would that mean for the rest of the design?

If you finish early

For each of these, write your prediction and your reasoning first, then test it and say whether you were right.

A. Put more cars on the train in Setup. Does your smallest lift hill change?

B. Turn Friction on. What happens to your answer, and why?

C. Keeping your lift hill exactly as it is, can you change anything else about the coaster so that the train gets round the loop more comfortably? What did you try, and what happened?
