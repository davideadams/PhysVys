Organic Molecule Builder

Info

Draw a carbon compound and see it written several ways: skeletal, structural and displayed formulae, a condensed formula, molecular and empirical formulae, SMILES, and a 3D model. The page also works out the IUPAC name and can show each step of the naming.

The naming covers alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, haloalkanes, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, amines and amides, without rings. When a molecule is outside that, the page says so and why, instead of guessing.

It follows the 2013 IUPAC recommendations: the longest chain comes first, even if a double bond ends up in a side chain (3-methylidenehexane); side chains are named like propan-2-yl and prop-2-en-1-yl; and locants are kept unless the rules drop them (prop-1-ene, propan-2-one, but ethanol). It uses the systematic names taught in school, such as ethanoic acid and methanal, rather than the retained names acetic acid and formaldehyde. E/Z and R/S prefixes are not assigned, but the page tells you when a name needs one.

The name starts hidden and fills in as you work through the naming steps. Turn off "Build the name in the steps" to see it straight away.

Practice
What does each stage include?

Stage 1: alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, haloalkanes, alcohols, carboxylic acids and esters. C=C and C≡C bonds appear only in alkenes and alkynes.

Stage 2: adds aldehydes, ketones, amines and amides, more molecules with two functional groups, and C=C bonds alongside functional groups.
